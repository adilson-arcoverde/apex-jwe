# 0008: Publish under Apache 2.0 rather than a noncommercial license

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-09-07
- Deciders: the author

## Context

The plan this repository came out of had settled on PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0 for every
project in the set, so that commercial use would require a separate license. That reasoning
holds for some of those projects. It does not hold for this one, and the difference is the
audience.

Nobody writes Apex as a hobby. An org costs money, and the people who need JWE in Apex are
integrating with a payment network, a bank or a government interface, which is to say they are
at work. PolyForm Noncommercial does permit government institutions, educational institutions
and charities, and government integrations are one of the places message level encryption gets
required, so the noncommercial audience here is not empty. It is small, though, and everyone
else who would reach for this library would be barred from it.

Two other things weighed on the decision. What this repository is worth is mostly reputational:
it is a dated, tested implementation of something the platform does not offer, and that value
grows with adoption and shrinks without it. And the money in work like this is in the consulting
around an integration rather than in license fees, which a noncommercial license actually
obstructs, because a consultancy cannot evaluate the library commercially before hiring anyone
to integrate it.

There is also where the code came from. The arithmetic underneath it descends from a gist
published freely in 2016, and three other projects built on the same one, one of them under
Apache 2.0. Closing that chain commercially would be within the author's rights and against the
grain of how it reached him.

## Decision

Apache License 2.0, for this repository. Licensing is decided per repository from now on, rather
than once for the whole set.

No license header in the source files. `LICENSE` carries the terms and `NOTICE` carries the
attribution, which is what Apache 2.0 requires; ten small classes do not each need three lines
of boilerplate at the top.

## Consequences

- Anyone can use this in commercial work, which is the point. There is no license to sell and
  no licensing correspondence to answer.
- The patent grant of section 3 comes along with it. For RSA and JWE, whose patents expired long
  ago, that costs nothing and closes a question a cautious legal team would otherwise ask.
- Section 4 requires a redistributor to preserve `NOTICE`, which is how the credit to the 2016
  lineage travels with the code. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` records the detail.
- Contributions arrive under the same license by section 5, so no contributor agreement is
  needed. Selling a commercial license later would require the agreement of everyone who
  contributed, and that door closes as soon as the first external pull request lands. That is
  accepted.
- The other repositories in the plan keep their own decision. A Python tool with a real
  personal and nonprofit audience is a different question from an Apex library for enterprise
  integrations.
